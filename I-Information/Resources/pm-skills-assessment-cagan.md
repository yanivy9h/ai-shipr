# PM Skills Assessment (Cagan / SVPG)

A competency model and coaching method for assessing and developing a product manager across 16 skills. Used as the rubric behind PM-Coach's **Leveling & Growth** mode.

> **Framework:** Marty Cagan / SVPG (Silicon Valley Product Group).
> **Adaptation this resource is based on:** the work of **Nurit Gazit**.

---

## How to use it (the coaching method)

1. **Make the gap analysis.** For each of the 16 skills, assign two ratings on a 1-10 scale:
   - **Importance** — where this PM needs to be *for their current role and level*.
   - **Current capability** — where they actually perform today.
   - **Gap** = Importance minus Current. A gap only matters when it sits on a high-importance skill.
2. **Focus on the 3 biggest gaps.** Do not try to close everything at once. Rank by gap size weighted by importance. Example: a skill rated importance 9 with current capability 5 is a significant, high-priority gap; a skill rated importance 5 with current capability 3 is not.
3. **Coach.** For each of the top 3, define concrete actions: coaching, training, reading, or exercises. See Coaching Tools below.
4. **Meet weekly.** Sit down no less than once a week to discuss progress on the plan.
5. **Next phase.** Once the gaps are closed, show how the importance ratings shift for the *next level* (or a different role). Those become the new development targets and the basis for a promotion case.

**Remember:**
- Expectations change with the level of responsibility. The importance ratings below are illustrative defaults, not fixed. Re-set them per PM and per level.
- Not all skills are equally important.
- Not all gaps are equally significant.

## The 16 skills

Importance values in parentheses are Cagan's example weights. Reset them for the specific role before assessing.

### People Skills and Responsibilities

| Skill | Imp. | Assessment question |
|---|---|---|
| Product Sense | 10 | Does the PM consistently make high-quality product decisions by combining user empathy, clear problem definition, and sound judgment about trade-offs, feasibility, and business impact? |
| Team Collaboration Skills | 10 | Does the PM work collaboratively with engineers and designer, with mutual respect, involving them early and giving them direct access to customers? |
| Evangelism Skills | 9 | Can the PM effectively share the product vision and strategy, and motivate the team, stakeholders, and others who must contribute? |
| Time Management Skills | 8 | Does the PM protect time for the critically important work rather than losing most of it to daily firefighting? |
| Stakeholder Management Skills | 7 | Do stakeholders feel they have a true product partner? Has the PM built mutual respect and trust across the company, including senior leadership? |
| Leadership Skills | 6 | Though the PM manages no one, do the team and stakeholders look to her for leadership, especially under stress? |

### Process Skills and Techniques

| Skill | Imp. | Assessment question |
|---|---|---|
| Product Delivery | 9 | Does the PM understand her supporting role in delivery and her responsibilities to engineers and product marketing once discovery is done? |
| Research and Ideation | 9 | Does the PM understand the context (customer needs, technology, competitive landscape) well enough to do meaningful work, backed by evidence rather than assumption? |
| Product Development Process | 7 | Does the PM have a solid grasp of the full development process — discovery, delivery, and her product-owner administrative responsibilities? |
| Product Discovery Techniques & Processes | 7 | Does the PM know how to tackle product risks up front, solve problems collaboratively, focus on outcomes, and use both qualitative and quantitative techniques? |
| Holistic View of Product | 5 | Does the PM maintain a holistic, end-to-end view of the product experience? |

### Product Knowledge

| Skill | Imp. | Assessment question |
|---|---|---|
| User and Customer Knowledge | 10 | Is the PM a company-acknowledged expert on the target users/customers and how they discover, evaluate, buy, and gain value from the product? |
| Industry and Domain Knowledge | 10 | Is the PM knowledgeable about the industry and domain, the competitive landscape, and relevant trends and their implications? |
| Product Operational Knowledge | 10 | Is the PM an acknowledged expert on how the product actually works — able to demo, train a new customer, and handle live support inquiries? |
| Data Knowledge | 9 | Is the PM skilled with the data tools and an acknowledged expert on how the product is actually used and its impact on the business? |
| Business and Company Knowledge | 7 | Does the PM understand the dimensions of the business (marketing, sales, finance, legal, privacy) enough to be a trustworthy partner to each stakeholder? |
