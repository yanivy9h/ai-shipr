Functional Personas
What they're trying to do — job-to-be-done, emotional drivers, biggest pain, and what success actually looks like for each user.
M
Maya
The Casual Learner
Casual
Job to be done
Learn enough Spanish to order food and not feel helpless in markets — without it feeling like homework after a long day.
Biggest pain
The streak breaking feels final. Missing one day spirals into guilt, not motivation. She doesn't think of herself as "a language person" — any failure confirms that belief.
Emotional drivers
Fear of embarrassment abroad Small visible wins Avoids guilt Progress bars
What success looks like
She finishes a 4-min lesson on the tube with no WiFi. A notification lands at 9:15pm — the exact moment she'd open the app. She doesn't associate Duolingo with guilt.
Key product hypothesis
If we remove the permanence of streak loss (soft reset, grace period), Maya's churn on day 10–14 will drop significantly.
A
Alex
The Motivated Returnee
Serious
Job to be done
Build a Japanese habit that actually sticks this time — not just survive week two, but build the consistency he has with his morning run.
Biggest pain
He knows the first two weeks are the easy part. His fear is the first streak loss during travel — it happened before and he never came back. He needs a safety net, not perfection.
Emotional drivers
System & process Progress visibility Compares to past self Consistency pride
What success looks like
He loses his streak during a work trip to Amsterdam. The app offers a one-time repair. He takes it, completes a lesson from the hotel, and the habit survives. He reaches day 90.
Key product hypothesis
If we offer a streak-repair mechanism triggered specifically by travel detection or multi-day gap, Alex-type users will retain past day 30 at a measurably higher rate.
C
Carol
The Streak Purist
Power user
Job to be done
Maintain her streak and feel validated that she is the kind of person who follows through on things.
Biggest pain
She doesn't have a product problem — she has a social one. She does her lesson every day but does it alone. A colleague laughed it off. She'd love to know a friend is also on a streak.
Emotional drivers
Streak = identity Quiet pride No competition Belonging
What success looks like
She can see a friend's streak next to hers. No leaderboard, no competition — just awareness. It gives her something to mention at lunch: "Did you do your French today?"
Key product hypothesis
If we surface a friend's streak in a low-pressure, non-competitive format, Carol-type users will increase word-of-mouth referrals and extend their own LTV.
